EPISODE 0 • ORIGIN

HORIZON

The true beginning of The Stopped Watch

One ordinary morning. A car left at a local shop for routine maintenance. A mild stroke during a normal test drive. Nine vehicles involved.

And on the asphalt — a Tissot watch that was supposed to be in the center console, still ticking.

“I painted Horizon long before I understood what it was saying.
A dark figure. An open sky. Someone standing at the edge of something.”

From that silence, Lolo Tagapu was born — the quiet collector of stopped watches, and the voice of a simple rule that could save lives.

EPISODE 0 • THE TRUE STORY

What Really Happened

The car was brought in for an oil change and a check of the clutch and braking system. The owner left for a meeting thirty minutes away, planning to pick it up afterward.

After service, the mechanic’s brother took the vehicle out for a standard test drive — normal shop protocol.

No one knew a mild stroke was about to occur.

He struck the first vehicle. Stopped. Felt threatened by bystanders. In confusion and panic he drove on, heading back toward the shop. The chain continued until nine vehicles were involved.

His later account: he was running to survive because he felt his life was threatened.

One second. One medical event.
Nine vehicles. One watch still ticking on the asphalt.
EPISODE 0 • THE MEANING

The Watch That Kept Beating

Among the debris was the owner’s Tissot — the watch that had been sitting in the center console. It was still ticking.

That image became the heart of the project: time that continues even after chaos. A heartbeat that refuses to stop.

From the original painting HORIZON and from that day, the figure of Lolo Tagapu fully formed — an old man who collects stopped watches “for honor,” and who speaks only one quiet warning:

“10 seconds lang, anak.
Pause. Look. Wait.”

This episode is not only remembrance. It is a warning offered so that others may still come home.
